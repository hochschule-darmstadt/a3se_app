# Ab in den Urlaub Comparative UX Review

- Status: draft
- Owner: Requirements/UX
- Last reviewed: 2026-08-14

## Provenance and limitation

The public German travel portal [ab-in-den-urlaub.de](https://www.ab-in-den-urlaub.de/) was inspected on 2026-08-14 at stakeholder request as comparative UX evidence. The site is neither a stakeholder nor an authoritative source for this product. Its current content, commercial model, and interaction may change, and inaccessible or dynamic booking steps were not treated as observed facts.

## Observed patterns

- The public entry page supports broad discovery through prominent travel categories, destinations, deal cards, prices, and a booking/search entry.
- Account access (`Mein Konto`), favourites, and telephone assistance are visible alongside discovery rather than replacing it.
- Search/discovery content exposes representative price, duration, party size, board, transport, and rating information before booking.
- The public description presents a short journey of entering destination, dates, and party size; choosing an available offer; booking online; and receiving travel documents by email.
- Search refinement and filtering are emphasized as part of finding a suitable offer.

## Adopted and rejected inspiration

The proposed customer navigation adopts a recognisable discovery funnel—search, results, detail, composition, offer, order—and keeps account and assistance reachable as supporting concerns. It does not copy the site's taxonomy, content volume, favourites, cashback, ratings, travel categories, email delivery, legal claims, or commercial checkout because those capabilities lack project requirements evidence.

The stakeholder additionally decided on 2026-08-14 that registration or sign-in is required no later than presentation of a Sales Offer. This decision is authoritative through the updated requirements, not through the comparison site.

## Design-system inspiration

A later visual review observed a strong dark-navy header, a blue/cyan travel-search emphasis, warm high-contrast call-to-action accents, large destination imagery, white content surfaces, compact benefit badges, and card-based offer groupings. The future customer profile may use these as directional inspiration for hierarchy, confidence, discovery, and conversion while establishing original tokens and branding. The staff profile may share semantic colours, typography, focus treatment, controls, and state meanings, but shall not inherit promotional density, decorative imagery, or sales-card layouts where they reduce operational efficiency.

Exact colours, typography, logos, images, promotional badges, commercial claims, cookie presentation, and proprietary layouts are not adopted. Accessibility, responsive behaviour, project requirements, and original identity take precedence over visual similarity.
